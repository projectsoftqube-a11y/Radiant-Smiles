## After Tooth Extraction Care

After a tooth is removed, your first job is to protect the blood clot that forms in the socket. The clot is what lets the area heal.

1. **Bite on the gauze.** Keep firm pressure on the gauze pad for 30 to 45 minutes so a clot can form. If the area is still bleeding after that, place a fresh pad and bite down for another 30 to 45 minutes.
2. **Protect the clot for 72 hours.** For the first 72 hours, don't rinse vigorously, drink through a straw, smoke, drink alcohol or brush the teeth next to the extraction site.
3. **Rest for a day.** Limit vigorous exercise for the next 24 hours.
4. **Use ice for swelling.** An ice pack on the outside of your face helps keep swelling down. Swelling usually goes down after 48 hours.
5. **Get back to your routine.** Resume brushing and flossing the rest of your mouth after 24 hours, keeping away from the extraction site until the 72 hours have passed. Most people return to normal activities after a few days.

## Care After a Dental Filling

Tooth-colored composite fillings are fully set when you leave the office, so you can chew on them as soon as the numbness wears off.

- **While you're numb:** the anesthetic usually lasts several hours. Avoid chewing and hot drinks until it wears off, so you don't bite your cheek or burn yourself.
- **Sensitivity:** some sensitivity to hot, cold and pressure is normal at first.
- **Discomfort:** a mild over-the-counter pain reliever, such as ibuprofen or acetaminophen (Tylenol), can help. Follow the directions on the label.

## After a Crown or Bridge

Crowns and bridges usually take at least two appointments. Between visits, a temporary crown protects your tooth while the final one is made.

- **Treat the temporary gently.** Temporary crowns can come loose. Avoid sticky foods and gum, and chew on the other side of your mouth.
- **Keep cleaning.** Brush normally. When you floss, slide the floss out from the side rather than pulling it up, so the temporary stays in place.
- **Expect some sensitivity.** Sensitivity to temperature and pressure is normal. It should ease a few weeks after the final crown or bridge is placed.

## Care After a Root Canal

Most patients can drive themselves home and get back to normal activities straight after a root canal. Root canals are often completed over two appointments.

- **Keep your follow-up visit.** The treated tooth needs a final restoration, usually a crown, within a few weeks.
- **Follow the temporary crown advice above** if you have a temporary in place: avoid sticky foods and gum, and chew on the other side.

## After Cosmetic Dental Reconstruction

New veneers, crowns or other cosmetic work can take a little getting used to.

- **Your bite:** it takes time to adjust to the feel of your new bite.
- **Sensitivity:** some hot and cold sensitivity is normal.
- **Sore gums:** rinse with warm salt water (a teaspoon of salt in a cup of warm water) three times a day. A mild pain reliever, such as acetaminophen (Tylenol) or ibuprofen, can also help.
- **Speech and saliva:** your speech may feel different at first, and you may have more saliva than usual. Both usually settle in about a week.
- **Protect the results:** brush and floss daily. Avoid hard foods such as ice, nuts and peanut brittle, and sticky candy. Smoking stains new teeth, and coffee, tea, red wine and berries can too, so keep them to a minimum.
- **Guards:** if you play sports, wear a custom mouthguard. If you grind your teeth, wear the night guard we provide.

## Home Care FAQs

### How long should I bite on gauze after a tooth extraction?
Bite firmly on the gauze pad for 30 to 45 minutes after a tooth extraction so a blood clot can form in the socket. If the area is still bleeding after that, replace it with a fresh pad and bite down again. Call (215) 860-4600 if heavy bleeding continues.

### When can I brush my teeth after an extraction?
You can brush and floss the rest of your mouth after 24 hours. Keep the toothbrush away from the extraction site for 72 hours, and for the same 72 hours avoid vigorous rinsing, straws, smoking and alcohol, which can disturb the blood clot.

### Is it normal for a new filling to feel sensitive?
Yes. Some sensitivity to hot, cold and pressure is normal after a composite filling. The filling is fully set when you leave the office, so you can chew normally once the numbness wears off. Avoid chewing and hot drinks while you're still numb, usually for several hours.
