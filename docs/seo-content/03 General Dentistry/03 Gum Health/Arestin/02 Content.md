## What Arestin Is

Arestin is the brand name for minocycline hydrochloride microspheres, an antibiotic that kills the bacteria behind gum disease infections. Instead of a pill you swallow, the medicine is placed straight into the pockets between your gums and teeth, where the bacteria live.

That's why it's often described as an antibiotic in the gum pocket. The microspheres stay in the pocket and release the antibiotic there, so the medicine works on the infected area itself.

---

## When Arestin Treatment Is Used

Arestin treatment is used after a [deep teeth cleaning](/preventative-care/deep-teeth-cleaning/), also called scaling and root planing. The deep cleaning removes tartar and bacteria from below the gumline. Arestin is then placed in pockets that are deep or infected, to help fight the bacteria that remain.

The dentist may recommend Arestin if:

- You have gum disease with deep gum pockets
- Some pockets are still deep after a deep cleaning

It isn't a replacement for a deep cleaning. It works together with one.

---

## What to Expect

Getting Arestin is a short added step at the end of your deep cleaning.

1. **Deep cleaning:** the dentist cleans below the gumline and smooths the root surfaces.
2. **Placement:** Arestin is placed directly into each affected pocket.
3. **Aftercare:** you go home with instructions for the treated areas.
4. **Follow-up:** at a later visit, the dentist rechecks your pocket depths to see how your gums are healing.

There's no pill to swallow: the medicine goes straight to the infected pockets.

### Aftercare tips
According to Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center's patient information on this medicine, you should avoid chewing hard, crunchy or sticky foods with the treated teeth for 1 week after treatment, and avoid touching the treated area. Follow the dentist's instructions on when to brush and floss near the treated teeth.

---

## Who Shouldn't Have Arestin

Arestin isn't right for everyone. Tell the dentist before treatment if you:

- Are allergic to minocycline or any other medicine
- Are pregnant, or might be, because the medicine may harm an unborn baby
- Are asking about treatment for a child: it's not usually used in children younger than 8, because it can discolor developing teeth

Also tell the dentist about any other medicines you take.
