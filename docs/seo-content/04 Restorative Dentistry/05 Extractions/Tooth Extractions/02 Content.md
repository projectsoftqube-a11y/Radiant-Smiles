## When a Tooth Needs to Come Out

Your dentist will try to save a tooth first, and removes it only when keeping it would cause more harm. Common reasons for an extraction include:

- **Severe decay** that has destroyed too much of the tooth to repair
- **Advanced gum disease** that has loosened the tooth in the bone
- **A tooth broken beyond repair,** for example below the gum line
- **A poorly positioned or impacted tooth,** such as a wisdom tooth that can't come in properly
- **Preparation for orthodontic treatment,** when a tooth needs to be removed to make room

If a tooth can be saved, a [root canal](/restorative-dentistry/root-canal/) and crown is often the better long-term choice. Your dentist will show you the X-rays and explain the options either way.

## What to Expect During a Tooth Extraction

You'll know what's happening at each step of an extraction. Before the appointment, tell your dentist about any medicines you take and any health conditions.

1. **Numbing.** A local anesthetic numbs the tooth, the jawbone and the gums around it.
2. **Loosening the tooth.** The dentist gently rocks the tooth to widen the socket. You'll feel firm pressure but shouldn't feel sharp pain. If you do, tell the team straight away and more anesthetic can be given.
3. **Removing the tooth.** Once the socket is wide enough, the tooth is lifted out.
4. **Gauze and instructions.** You bite on gauze to control bleeding and go home with aftercare instructions.

### When a tooth is removed in sections
Some teeth are firmly anchored or have curved roots that make them hard to lift out whole. In that case the dentist cuts the tooth into sections and removes it piece by piece.

If you're anxious, ask about sedation options when you book. You can also bring headphones and music.

## Tooth Extraction Recovery

Most people return to normal activities within a few days of a simple extraction. The first 72 hours matter most, because a blood clot needs to form and stay in the socket so it can heal.

### The first hour
Bite firmly on the gauze for 30 to 45 minutes. If bleeding continues, replace it with fresh gauze and bite down again.

### The first 72 hours
For three days after your extraction, avoid:

- Rinsing vigorously
- Drinking through a straw
- Smoking
- Drinking alcohol
- Brushing right next to the extraction site

Limit vigorous exercise for the first 24 hours. An ice pack on the outside of your cheek helps keep swelling down. You can go back to your normal brushing and flossing after 24 hours, while still keeping away from the extraction site.

Call the office if bleeding won't stop, or if pain or swelling gets worse after the first few days. Our [home care instructions](/patient-information/care-and-comfort/home-instructions/) cover aftercare in more detail.

## Replacing the Tooth After an Extraction

Replacing an extracted tooth keeps your bite working and stops the neighboring teeth drifting into the gap. It's worth planning the replacement before the tooth comes out, because some options start at the extraction visit.

- **[Dental implants](/restorative-dentistry/dental-implants/):** a titanium post that replaces the root. In some cases the implant can be placed at the same appointment as the extraction. Our current offer is $500 off the regular $3,500 for an implant, abutment and crown.
- **[Dental bridges](/restorative-dentistry/dental-bridges/):** a fixed replacement tooth supported by the teeth beside the gap.
- **[Partial dentures](/restorative-dentistry/dentures/partial-dentures/):** a removable option that can fill one or several gaps.
- **[Immediate dentures](/restorative-dentistry/dentures/immediate-dentures/):** if all your remaining teeth are coming out, a denture made in advance can be placed the same day.

## Tooth Extraction FAQs

### Does a tooth extraction hurt?
A local anesthetic numbs the tooth, jawbone and gums first, so you should feel pressure as the tooth is loosened but not sharp pain. If you feel pain at any point, tell the team and more anesthetic can be given. Some soreness afterward is normal, and an ice pack helps with swelling.

### How long does it take to recover from a tooth extraction?
Most people return to normal activities within a few days. The first 72 hours matter most: avoid straws, smoking, alcohol, vigorous rinsing and brushing next to the site. You can resume normal brushing elsewhere after 24 hours, and limit vigorous exercise for the first day.

### Can I have a tooth pulled the same day I call?
Sometimes. Radiant Smiles @ Floral Vale keeps same-day emergency slots open every business day for a 30-minute limited exam that diagnoses the problem and begins treatment. Whether the tooth comes out that day depends on what the exam finds. Call (215) 860-4600 early in the day.

### Should I replace a tooth after it's pulled?
In most cases, yes. A gap lets neighboring teeth drift and makes chewing harder on that side. Options include a dental implant, a bridge, a partial denture or, if all teeth are coming out, an immediate denture. Planning before the extraction gives you the most choice.
